# Vision

## One-liner
Help kids play English-first games without needing a parent translator.

## Problem
Many popular games for ages 5–10 assume native English comprehension. Kids frequently stop play to ask a parent to interpret UI text, quest goals, or prompts. Parents may not know the game context, leading to frustration and churn.

## Target users
- Primary: children ages 5–10 playing PC games on Windows
- Secondary: parents who want the child to be more independent

## Core job-to-be-done
“When I don’t understand what the game wants, I can ask and quickly get a simple answer that matches what’s on my screen.”

## Product principles
1. **Fast enough to stay in flow**: answers should arrive quickly; user should not feel like they ‘left the game’.
2. **One-handed simple**: hotkeys and minimal UI; child-friendly defaults.
3. **Explain like I’m 7**: the default response style should be short, actionable, and age-appropriate.
4. **Failure is graceful**: when anything fails, the app should say what happened and what to do next.
5. **Privacy by default**: local logs only, no PII, explicit opt-in for telemetry.

## Non-goals (for now)
- Full game translation/localization
- Competitive multiplayer coaching/strategy
- Console support (Xbox/PlayStation)

## Success (initial)
- A friend can install and get a successful question-answer within 10 minutes.
- In a 30-minute session, the app has no crashes and minimal “I don’t know” responses.
